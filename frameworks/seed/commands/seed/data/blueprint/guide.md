# Blueprint — Conversation Guide

The Deep Dive for one phase of a Blueprint. The owner and their operations seat show the
machine the real business, one opportunity at a time, until the build's shape is genuinely
understood. Level one throughout: a few questions at a time, their words quoted back, nothing
guessed. The access map (`ACCESS-MAP.md`) is written BEFORE this conversation starts.

## Section 1: The job as it happens today
**Explore:** Walk me through it from the first trigger to the last click. What tells you it is time to do it? Which screens do you touch, in what order? Show me.
**Suggest:** Ask them to open the real software and narrate while you write the steps down in order, in their words. Ask for the documents that exist (SOPs, checklists, templates, example files) and have them drop copies into `context/phase-N/`.
**Depth:** required

## Section 2: Decisions and exceptions
**Explore:** What decisions happen in the middle, and what makes you go one way or the other? Where does it go wrong, and what do you do then?
**Suggest:** Ask for the three most common exceptions and one rare one. Each exception is either a rule the machine can follow or a place a person stays in the loop; decide which, out loud.
**Depth:** required

## Section 3: People and checks
**Explore:** Who else touches it? Who checks it? Who owns it today and approves the machine's work on it?
**Suggest:** Name one owner for the opportunity. If nobody owns it, say so plainly; the owner decides who will.
**Depth:** required

## Section 4: What must never change
**Explore:** The voice, a format, an approval step, a customer who is special. And the constraints the session recorded for this opportunity.
**Suggest:** Read the session's constraints back by name. Ask "what would make you angry if the machine changed it?" and write those down as constraints too.
**Depth:** required

## Section 5: The numbers
**Explore:** How long does this take today, how often, how many? How would you know it worked?
**Suggest:** Every "no number known" gets a real number now or a week-one measurement plan: what to count, who counts it, for how long. Never guess a number.
**Depth:** required

## Section 6: The design
**Explore:** What goes in. What the machine does, step by step. What comes out. Where the human stays in the loop, and what stays human for good.
**Suggest:** For each piece, name its home: their existing tool first, an always-on rail only when it must run without the machine, a proper database only when data needs a durable home, a plain script when a script is enough. Apply the simplicity test both ways. State the sandbox rule: the old way keeps running until the owner retires it. Phase 1 must show a working result inside seven days of starting the build; split it if it is bigger.
**Depth:** required

## Section 7: Access and cards
**Explore:** Which systems does this design touch, and who grants access to each (from `ACCESS-MAP.md`)? What will the person have to do that the machine cannot?
**Suggest:** List the cards this design needs, each addressed to the named grantor, five steps at most. Keys never pass through chat: a card names the file or screen where the person types the key themselves.
**Depth:** required

## Section 8: Verification
**Explore:** On what real work, this week, will this be proven? Who watches? Which scoreboard rows must move?
**Suggest:** Every verification ends with a named person seeing the real result. The machine's own success signal is never proof. An always-on piece does not exist until it has fired on a real event a person saw.
**Depth:** required

## Section 9: Ideas that came up
**Explore:** Anything new the owner raised while we looked at the real thing.
**Suggest:** Write each one into `amendments.md` with the date and their words, place it in a phase, and say where it landed. Never refuse one, never derail this phase for one.
**Depth:** optional
