# The hand-off card

The one shape your Caddy uses when a step needs a person. It prepares everything first: the
file with the placeholder already written, the exact values to type, the link to open, the
reason in one line. One card at a time. Five steps at most. Addressed to the person the
access map names. Keys never pass through chat: the card names the file or screen where the
person types the key themselves, and your Caddy proves the connection works without ever
reading the value. After every card, the real result is verified with the person watching.

> **Your turn (about 3 minutes)**
> 1. Open this link: [link]
> 2. Click "Create account" and use [business email].
> 3. When you see the dashboard, come back here and type: done
>
> Why: the tool needs an account only a person can open.
> When you're back, I check that it worked before we go on.

Rules the card always follows:

- A time estimate in the title, honest and rough
- Numbered steps, each one a single action, never more than five
- The last step always says what to type when they are back
- One line of why, in plain words
- One line saying the machine checks the result before going on
- Recorded afterward in the phase's design record, and in `SYSTEMS.md` when it touched
  something outside the machine

## When several cards are waiting

Cards pile up when a phase needs several people or several accounts. Respect the owner's time:

- Say the count and the total time first: "I have three cards for you this week, about twelve
  minutes in all. All now, or one a day?" Then do what they say.
- When the owner says how long they have ("I have ten minutes"), pick the cards that fit, biggest
  unblock first, and say what waits: "Two fit in ten minutes; the third waits for tomorrow."
- When nothing fits, say so and offer the smallest card anyway: "None of them fits in two
  minutes; the shortest is four. Now or later?"
- One card at a time still holds: the next card is handed over only after the last one is
  verified.
- Zero or one card waiting: no counting, no batching talk, just the card.
- Every card handed over gets a dated line in `WAITING-ON-YOU.md`: one line per item, removed the
  moment it is verified done; a card handed over again keeps its first date.

## A card for someone who is not in the room

When the access map names a person other than the one you are talking to, write the card so the
owner can forward it unchanged: a one-line opener in the owner's name, then the card.

> [Owner first name] asked me to set up [the thing], and you hold the keys to [system]. Here is
> the one thing I need from you:
>
> **Your turn (about 3 minutes)**
> 1. Open this link: [link]
> 2. Click "Add member" and use [the business email].
> 3. When you see it listed, tell [owner first name] you are done.
>
> Why: the tool needs someone with your access to say yes.
> [Owner first name]'s Caddy checks that it worked before going on.

The owner sends it (a text, an email, a message in their own tool); you never do. Record it as
"sent to [name] by [owner] on [date]" in the design record and in `WAITING-ON-YOU.md`.

## A card that asks someone to sign up or pay

Before any card that opens an account or a paid plan, open the vendor's current pricing page
yourself. Put in the card the plan the design needs, its price and the date you read it: "the
Starter plan, $20 a month as of [date], on their pricing page", in whatever currency the page
shows. Name the one thing the plan must
include (the API, the team seat, the connector) so nobody signs up for the tier that lacks it. If
you could not read the page, say exactly that in the card ("I could not read the price today; it
is on the page in step 1") and never guess a number.
