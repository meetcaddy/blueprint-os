# /draft: in the owner's voice, never sent

## Before drafting

1. Read `BUSINESS.md` for the business, the team and the vocabulary. Use their words.
2. Read `VOICE.md` in this folder if it exists. If it does not, ask once, with one card:

   > **Your turn (about 5 minutes)**
   > 1. Find three things you wrote yourself and are happy with: an email, a message, a post.
   > 2. Paste them into a new file called `VOICE.md` in this folder (I made an empty one).
   >    Add one line at the top: "Things I never say: ..."
   > 3. Come back here and type: done
   >
   > Why: I write like you only if I have seen how you write.
   > When you're back, I draft from it.

   Create the empty `VOICE.md` before handing the card over. If the owner would rather
   draft without it, draft plainly and say the draft will sound more like them once
   `VOICE.md` exists.

## Inputs

- What they asked for (the topic, and the audience or length if they named one)
- `VOICE.md`: the tone, the openers, the sign-offs, the phrases, the "never say" list
- `BUSINESS.md`: what the business is, their words for things

## How to draft

- Match their voice. A generic AI voice is the failure.
- Honor every "never say" line.
- No em dashes, no spaced double hyphens. Commas, colons, periods, parentheses.
- Never invent a fact, a date, a price, a name or a commitment they did not give. If the ask
  is too thin to draft cleanly, draft the best version from what is there and leave a
  bracket where a fact belongs: [date].
- Short over long. They hit send; write nothing they would have to delete.

## Output

The draft only. No preamble, no commentary. One draft per ask unless they ask for another.
It stays in the conversation; nothing is written to disk and nothing is sent. Every message
that leaves the business is the owner's to send (guardrail 5).
